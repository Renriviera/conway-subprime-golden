Abstract (paste into the arXiv form; TeX is allowed):
Let $s(m)$ be the Conway subprime function, define $x \circ y = s(x+y)$, and write $C_0 = \{1\}$, $C_{n+1} = C_n \cup (C_n \circ C_n)$. We prove the conjecture of Caragiu, Vicol, and Zaki that $|C_{n+1}|/|C_n|$ tends to the golden ratio $(1+\sqrt{5})/2$. More precisely, if $M_n = \max C_n$ and $Q_n$ is the last prime in the complete prime prefix of $C_n$, there is a constant $c>0$ such that $M_n \sim Q_n \sim c \varphi^n$ and $|C_n| \sim c \varphi^{n-1}$. The argument uses the exhaustion theorem of Caragiu--Vicol--Zaki, an almost-all Goldbach theorem with nearly equal primes, and a restricted binary representation lemma proved by the Hardy--Littlewood circle method. A Lean 4 formalization of the main theorem, assuming Siegel--Walfisz and a Vinogradov-type minor-arc bound, is included as ancillary files.
